# Native meter

Operate surface: Windows C# / .NET 10 / WPF. The Lone Trail v1.0 materials guide the orbital theme. The four pages are live, history, enemy intelligence and connection settings. The reference and initial size are 1672×941 DIPs, with a 560×540 minimum. First launch has one Damage/Totals/Recent DPS panel and no HUD.

## Composition and skins

The common WPF templates and dynamic brush resources support three skins. Default 孤星 uses a dark slate workspace, metallic edges, orange selection and cyan team DPS. 人们，我们。 (People, A People) uses red accents, warm paper, dark steel and city artwork. 巴别塔 (Babel) pairs an ivory fractured-tower masthead with charcoal records, fine battle-time ticks and restrained crimson selection.

The sidebar opens the full enemy page. The live summary uses the shared dark panel surface. Battle and instance identity preserve selection across HP, transformation and rebirth updates. Current HP, current form MaxHP, recorded phase capacity and the separate stage estimate retain their own evidence. A known lower bound uses ≥ and leaves the HP ratio blank. Enemy chart points distinguish exact HP, lower bounds and gaps. Effective damage starts at the enemy observation baseline, with other native sources listed separately.

The overview leads with team near-five-second DPS and a 60-game-second trend. Overview figures use whole numbers. At 1320 DIPs and above, the alloy-framed overview spans the workspace. The enemy summary, ranking and compact inspector fill the remaining height. At 1100–1319 DIPs the summary moves above the ranking and inspector. Smaller windows collapse navigation labels and stack the live panels. Keyboard and pointer can inspect the same chart samples. Missing samples touch the baseline with a dotted trace and keep their unavailable reading on inspection.

The inspector reads the pinned operator independently of ranked visibility. The compact Lone Trail layout puts the portrait beside the first three readings, and presents overkill, mitigation and skill-period damage share as parallel rings below on-field time in the main data card. Each field explains its calculation on hover or keyboard focus. Its total/effective switch controls damage composition, engagement and skill split. Skill coverage divides observed skill-on time by certified presence. Passive skills count as non-skill and incomplete coverage keeps affected metrics unknown. History reads its independent selected record.

Skin changes update dynamic resources in place, without recreating windows or statistics, rewriting history, or touching HUD input flags/geometry/opacity. The same palette covers main, history, settings, tooltips, HUD and complete-ranking PNG. Persistence belongs to the existing version-3 layout owner, not a C# preferences file: see [settings and layout](docs/settings-and-layout.md).

## Typography, data and interaction

Cards use fine borders and skin-specific corner radii. Microsoft YaHei UI carries Chinese labels. Lone Trail uses bundled Barlow Semi Condensed for Latin labels and large readouts and IBM Plex Mono for aligned data. People, A People uses a serif heading style and aligned data figures. Ranking rows reuse keyed models. Hover holds row order while values update, and leaving restores the Python model's exact ranking. Keyboard navigation selects and pins rows. Full names remain available on hover.

Panel columns, tooltips, HUD and PNG use at most one decimal place (`0.#`). The operator detail inspector rounds every numeric value to a whole number and omits zero-valued rows. It shows effective damage, overkill damage and overkill rate separately, never the old "damage · includes overkill" wording, and does not repeat burst damage/overkill as separate rows because they are folded into the effective/overkill totals. Unknown remains an em dash, never zero; waiting, paused, stale, degraded and disconnected states have text, not just color.

Offline portraits retain their gray variant for retired operators and a name-badge fallback for unknown IDs. The pin survives disappearance from the recent ranking and clears at new battle identity. Damage bar length and share always follow the selected ranking basis, independent of the rate column. PNG renders all ranked rows without list virtualization or viewport clipping; export freezes the selected record/configuration/skin before the native save dialog. JSON remains the unchanged BattleRecord contract.

Each skin assigns a distinct color to physical, arts, elemental and true damage. Text legends, direct component names and exact-value reports identify each component. Healing uses green and shields use blue-gray. Thin segmented bars have surface gaps and exact-value hover content. Body and control contrast is checked against both palettes.

## HUD and recovery

HUD is a native, topmost WPF window with adjustable width (initial 568 DIPs) and a height sized to the number of displayed operators, up to five 46-DIP rows. Additional operators remain reachable by wheel or keyboard without a visible scrollbar. The HUD has the panel title row as its drag surface, a small × close action on the title row, and no separate app/header/status footer. Rows show rank, portrait, name, continuous damage-type bar, total, DPS and share; the title is the category total (总伤/总治疗/总护盾). Hovering a bar lists physical/arts/elemental/true/overkill values. Lock and 70–100% opacity controls remain beside the source panel; global unlock and main-window recovery are available from the tray. Locked input uses actual Win32 transparent/layered/no-activate styles, not only WPF hit-test suppression. Closing HUD leaves collection running; deleting the source closes its HUD. Closing the last HUD while the GUI is hidden restores the GUI. Closing the main window hides to tray, with a taskbar-minimize fallback when tray construction fails; explicit Exit waits for worker cleanup.

WPF uses a PerMonitorV2 manifest and persists physical HWND rectangles through the existing layout schema. Hidden/minimized/maximized frames do not replace the last normal GUI rectangle. Restore clamps to a reachable monitor work area. Desktop runtime/protocol/distribution and the honest offline/manual acceptance boundary live in [desktop runtime](docs/desktop-runtime.md); historical Tk screenshots do not certify this replacement UI.
